## From competitor to counterparty

The narrative of private credit disintermediating regional banks is well understood. Less understood is the extent to which regional banks have become funding counterparties to the same direct lenders competing for their former borrowers, through subscription lines and net asset value facilities.

This creates a second-order exposure that doesn't show up cleanly in commercial and industrial loan concentration metrics, because it's booked as lending to funds rather than to operating companies.

## Where concentration hides

A bottom-up map of disclosed fund-finance exposure across regional lenders shows meaningful dispersion: a handful of banks have fund-finance books approaching double digits as a share of total commercial lending, concentrated in a small number of large sponsor relationships.

## What we're watching next

The key monitorable is subscription-line utilization during any liquidity event in private credit, since that is the transmission channel back into bank balance sheets that most public disclosure doesn't isolate well today.
